Nothing in your menu bar goes missing.

When the menu bar fills up, macOS stops drawing icons — no warning, no overflow, no way to click them. Overflow gives those items somewhere to go.

Install

Takes about a minute.

  1. Download the latest release Grab Overflow.zip from Releases.
  2. Move it to Applications Unzip, then drag Overflow.app into your Applications folder.
  3. Open it the first time with a right-click Right-click the app and choose Open. Overflow is signed but not notarized, so a normal double-click is blocked once.
  4. Turn on click-through, if you want it Optional. In Settings, enable click-through to grant Accessibility so hidden items open their own menus directly.

Questions

Does Overflow need any permissions?

No. Finding, listing and hiding items all work with nothing granted.

Accessibility is optional and does exactly one thing: it lets a hidden item open its own menu when you click it. Decline it and Overflow instead brings the item back into the bar for eight seconds so you can click it yourself.

Why does it never ask for Screen Recording?

Other menu bar managers need it because they redraw other apps' status-item images, and capturing those pixels is a permission. Overflow draws each app's own bundle icon instead, so the question never comes up.

Why is the first launch blocked?

The app is signed with a developer certificate but isn't notarized, so macOS asks for confirmation once. Right-click the app and choose Open, and it won't ask again.

Some hidden items don't respond when I click them.

Items built on a standard menu open directly. A few apps handle the mouse event themselves and ignore anything else, so Overflow can't press them — it falls back to revealing the item in the bar so you can click it by hand.

This depends on how the app was built, not on where its icon sits.

There's a gap in my menu bar now.

Hiding works by pushing items past the edge of the screen, so the space they occupied has to go somewhere. Lower the hide amount in Settings to shrink it, or set it to zero.

Do I need a Mac with a notch?

No. The notch makes the problem worse by removing usable space, but any full menu bar drops items. Overflow works the same on external displays and older Macs.

Does it collect anything?

No. There's no analytics, no network access and no account. Nothing leaves your Mac.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It's free and MIT licensed — the full source is on GitHub.
